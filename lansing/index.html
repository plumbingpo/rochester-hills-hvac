Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Lansing

A little routine care makes all the difference between a system that lasts and one that quits in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a new filter every one to three months, at least two feet of open space around the outdoor condenser, a gentle rinse each spring for the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your equipment plays a big role. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of the year.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a miserable night.

Comfort Challenges Lansing Homeowners Face

Most local HVAC failures give warning signs before they happen. A breaker that trips more than once, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, a musty smell at startup. These are the early symptoms of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're a quick visit. Left alone, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
